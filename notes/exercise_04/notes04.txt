Exercise 04 - E-Textiles (LED Patches)

Goal:
- Create an e-textile patch that can be attached to clothing
- Gain experience with functional materials and soft circuits
- Learn to deal with unexpected setbacks

Task:
1. Design an e-textile patch pattern (I chose a rocket)
2. Connect min. 5 LEDs
3. Use conductive thread
4. Decide on a suitable circuit (series vs parallel)
5. Sew by hand

Materials used:
- Sewable 3V coin battery holder
- 5 Sewable LEDs
- Sewing Needle
- Conductive Thread (yarn)
- Fabric (dark grey base + blue star pattern cover)
- Scissors
- Multimeter (for measuring resistance of yarn)
- Paper template
- Nail polish (mentioned in manual, not used)

Observations / Measurements:
- Multimeter measurement of one piece of conductive yarn: 9.4 Ω
- Multimeter measurement of another piece of yarn: 20.3 Ω
- The conductive thread has notable resistance, but is still usable for low-power LEDs
- Series circuit: not all LEDs light up because the cumulative voltage drop
  and yarn resistance is too high for the 3V coin cell
- Parallel circuit: resistance is divided, voltage is roughly constant
  -> ALL LEDs light up

First Attempt (failed):
- The sewing went poorly because I had never sewn before
- The thread routing was messy
- In the end there was a short circuit, so the LEDs did not light up
- The cover fabric was also hard to attach

Second Attempt (successful):
- Re-cut the rocket with cleaner edges
- Decided on a parallel circuit so all LEDs would light up
- Sewed the battery holder first, then added the LEDs one by one
- Tested the conductive thread on the multimeter before sewing
- Used the dark fabric as the base and the blue star fabric as a cover
- All 5 LEDs light up at the end!
